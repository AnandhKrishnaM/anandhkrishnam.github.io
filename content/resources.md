Organised loosely by topic.

## Foundations of GR

- **Wald — *General Relativity*.** The standard graduate reference; rigorous and geometric.
- **Misner, Thorne & Wheeler — *Gravitation*.** Encyclopaedic; best for physical intuition.
- **Hawking & Ellis — *The Large Scale Structure of Space-Time*.** Global methods, causal structure, singularity theorems.
- **O'Neill — *Semi-Riemannian Geometry*.** The cleanest account of the underlying geometry.

## The Einstein equations as PDE

- **Choquet-Bruhat — *General Relativity and the Einstein Equations*.** The reference for the Cauchy problem.
- **Ringström — *The Cauchy Problem in General Relativity*.** Modern and self-contained.
- **Chruściel — *Elements of General Relativity*.** Excellent modern notes.

## Causal structure & global methods

- **Penrose — *Techniques of Differential Topology in Relativity*.**
- **Beem, Ehrlich & Easley — *Global Lorentzian Geometry*.**

## Black holes & perturbations

- **Chandrasekhar — *The Mathematical Theory of Black Holes*.**
- **Dafermos & Rodnianski — *Lectures on black holes and linear waves*** ([arXiv:0811.0354](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.0354)).

## Spinors & spin coefficients

- **Penrose & Rindler — *Spinors and Space-Time*, vols. 1–2.**
- **Stewart — *Advanced General Relativity*.** A compact route to NP and GHP.

## Numerical relativity

- **Alcubierre — *Introduction to 3+1 Numerical Relativity*.**
- **Baumgarte & Shapiro — *Numerical Relativity*.**

## Lectures online

- [Frederic Schuller — GR lectures](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLFeEvEPtX_0S6vxxiiNPrJbLu9aK1UVC_) — extremely clear mathematical foundations
- [Sean Carroll — Lecture notes on general relativity](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9712019)
- [PIRSA — Perimeter Institute recorded seminars](https://pirsa.org)

## Tools

- **xAct** (Mathematica) — tensor computer algebra; what I use most.
- **Cadabra** — field-theory-friendly computer algebra.
- **SageManifolds** — symbolic differential geometry in Python.
- **Black Hole Perturbation Toolkit** — codes and data for perturbation theory.
